What does this tool do?

Paste in text and this tool scans it for four specific, well-defined stylistic patterns: a curated list of overused stock phrases and clichés, runs of consecutive sentences starting with the same word, unusually uniform sentence length across the whole text, and a higher-than-typical rate of em-dashes. Each match is highlighted directly in the text, and a summary panel breaks down counts by category — deliberately without any single combined score.

When should you use this tool?

  • Reviewing your own draft for generic phrasing before publishing
  • Spotting overused transition words that make writing feel formulaic
  • Checking whether a document leans on the same handful of stock phrases too often
  • Getting a second pair of eyes on sentence-length variety and rhythm

Frequently asked questions

Can this tell me if text was written by AI?
No. It flags stylistic patterns that show up often in generic or AI-generated writing, but human writers use every one of these patterns too, and AI-written text can easily avoid all of them. Treat results as a style-awareness signal, not a determination of authorship.
Why isn't there an overall "AI score" or percentage?
A single combined number would imply a precision this kind of heuristic checking can't honestly provide. Showing separate, explainable counts per pattern is more accurate about what's actually being measured than collapsing everything into one misleadingly confident figure.
What if my writing naturally has some of these patterns?
That's expected and normal — these patterns appear throughout ordinary human writing. A flag means "this pattern is present," not "this is a problem," and plenty of well-written text will still show a handful of matches.
Is my text uploaded to a server?
No, the entire process happens locally in your browser using JavaScript.
