What does this tool do?

This tool reorders a PDF's pages into booklet (saddle-stitch) imposition order — page 1 and the last page share one sheet, page 2 and the second-to-last page share the next, and so on — and lays two pages side by side on each landscape sheet. Print the result double-sided, fold each sheet in half, and stack them together: the pages read in the correct order from front cover to back.

When should you use this tool?

  • Printing a short report, program, or zine as a foldable, staple-in-the-middle booklet
  • Making a compact handout from a longer document without a print shop
  • Preparing a document for saddle-stitch binding at home or in an office
  • Creating an event program or menu that folds into a small booklet

Why Orisod

Imposition uses pdf-lib's page-embedding feature — the same vector-preserving technique N-Up PDF uses — so pages are scaled and repositioned without ever being rasterized. Text stays selectable in the finished booklet PDF.

Frequently asked questions

Why does my page count get padded with blank pages?
A booklet is built from full sheets, and each sheet holds exactly 4 pages (2 per side), so the total page count needs to be a multiple of 4. Blank pages are added at the end — they typically end up as blank space near the inside back cover, not disrupting your content's order.
Why "Flip on Short Edge" specifically?
The output sheets are landscape, and folding a landscape sheet in half along its short axis is what turns it into two portrait pages. Flip on Short Edge duplex printing keeps the front and back sides aligned correctly for that specific fold — Flip on Long Edge will print the back sides upside down relative to the front.
Is there a page limit?
No hard limit, but a very long document makes a very thick booklet that may not fold or staple neatly by hand — this works best for shorter documents (roughly under 60 pages).
Is my PDF uploaded to a server?
No, the entire process happens locally in your browser using JavaScript.
