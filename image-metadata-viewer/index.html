What is EXIF metadata?

Most photos from phones and cameras embed hidden data inside the file — called EXIF metadata. This can include the camera or phone model, the exact date and time the photo was taken, GPS coordinates, and shooting settings like aperture, shutter speed, and ISO. None of it is visible when you look at the photo, but it's there in the file.

When should you use this tool?

  • You want to check if a photo reveals a GPS location before sharing it
  • You're curious what camera or settings were used to take a photo
  • You need to verify the exact date and time a photo was taken
  • You're deciding whether a photo needs its metadata removed before posting

Frequently asked questions

Why does this photo show no metadata?
Many platforms (social media, messaging apps) strip EXIF data automatically when you upload or share a photo, so a re-downloaded copy often has none left.
Can I remove the metadata this tool shows?
Yes, use our EXIF Remover tool afterward to strip all detected metadata from the photo.
Is my photo uploaded to a server?
No, the entire process happens locally in your browser using JavaScript.
Does this work with PNG or WEBP files?
This tool is built for JPEG's standard EXIF format, which is by far the most common source of this kind of metadata. PNG and WEBP rarely carry EXIF data.
