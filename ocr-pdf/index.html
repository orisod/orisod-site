OCR PDF

Run OCR on a scanned PDF to make it searchable and selectable. Everything happens locally in your browser.

ℹ️ OCR is never 100% accurate. This reads each page as an image and recognizes the text on it, adding an invisible, searchable text layer on top of your original scan — the page still looks exactly the same, but you can now select, copy, and search its text. Recognition errors are more likely with low-resolution scans, unusual fonts, or handwriting. Proofread anything that matters (numbers, names, legal wording) rather than trusting the result blindly.
🔒 Processed on your device · No upload · No account · No watermark

What does this tool do?

This tool reads a scanned PDF page by page, recognizes the text on each page using OCR (optical character recognition), and rebuilds the PDF with the recognized text placed invisibly on top of the original page image. The page looks exactly like your original scan, but a PDF viewer can now find, select, and copy its text — something a plain scanned image can never do on its own. As a byproduct of the same OCR pass, you can also download the recognized text as a plain .txt file.

When should you use this tool?

  • You have a scanned document and need to search it for a specific word or phrase
  • You want to select and copy a passage from a scan instead of retyping it
  • Orisod's Extract PDF Text tool told you "no selectable text was found" — that's exactly the case this tool is for
  • You want the OCR'd text as a plain .txt file, not just embedded in the PDF

Frequently asked questions

Will the PDF look any different afterward?
No. The recognized text is added invisibly, on top of the exact same page image. Visually, the output PDF looks identical to what you uploaded.
How accurate is the OCR?
It depends heavily on scan quality. Clean, high-resolution, well-lit scans of printed text recognize very well; blurry photos, low resolution, unusual fonts, or handwriting will have more errors. Always proofread anything that matters.
Does this work with a large, many-page document?
Yes, there's no page limit, but OCR runs one page at a time and can take a while on longer documents — expect roughly half a second to a few seconds per page depending on your device. Keep the tab open while it runs.
Is my PDF uploaded to a server?
No, the entire process — rendering, OCR, and rebuilding the PDF — happens locally in your browser using JavaScript. Your file never leaves your device. The one exception is the first time you use this tool, when your browser downloads the OCR language data (a public, generic language model, not your file) and caches it for next time.
