How to Create a Favicon for Your Website (Free, No Design Skills)

That small icon in the browser tab next to your page title is called a favicon, and if you haven't set one up, your site is showing a generic blank icon where a bit of branding could be. Here's what a proper favicon actually requires, and how to generate one without touching design software.

What is a favicon, and why it matters

A favicon (short for "favorite icon") is the small square image that shows up in a browser tab, in bookmark lists, and on a phone's home screen if someone saves your site there. It's a small detail, but its absence is conspicuous — a generic icon or a broken image square next to your page title looks unfinished, especially when it's sitting next to a row of tabs from sites that all got this right. With a dozen tabs open, a recognizable favicon is often the only way visitors find their way back to your site without reading every tab title.

What a proper favicon package actually needs

Here's the part that surprises people: a "favicon" isn't just one file. Different browsers, operating systems, and devices each expect their own version:

  • favicon.ico — the classic format, still expected by many browsers as a fallback.
  • Multiple PNG sizes (typically 16×16, 32×32, 192×192, 512×512) — for different display contexts, from browser tabs to Android home screens.
  • apple-touch-icon — a specific size Apple devices use when a site is saved to an iPhone or iPad home screen.
  • A manifest reference — for progressive web apps, tying the icon set together.

Getting all of these right by hand means exporting your logo at half a dozen different sizes and naming each file exactly as expected — tedious even for someone comfortable with image editing software, and a real barrier if you're not.

Where to start: your logo or a simple image

You don't need a professional design — a clean, simple image works best anyway, since a favicon displays at a tiny size where fine detail disappears. A logo mark, an initial or two, or a simple icon representing your site all work well. Avoid anything with lots of small text or intricate detail; at 16×16 pixels, it'll just look like a blur.

Generating the full file set

A favicon generator tool takes a single source image and automatically produces every size and format a modern site needs — the .ico file, the various PNGs, and the Apple touch icon — packaged together so you're not manually exporting and renaming six separate files. You upload one image once and download the complete set.

Installing it on your site

Once you have the files, they need to be referenced in your site's HTML <head>, typically with a handful of <link> tags pointing to each file. Most favicon generators include these exact tags alongside the download, so it's a matter of copying them in rather than writing them from scratch.

Common favicon mistakes

  • Using a busy or detailed image. Anything with fine text or intricate shading turns into an unreadable smudge at 16×16 pixels — simpler is almost always better.
  • Only uploading one size. A single PNG might look fine in a browser tab but appear blank or oddly cropped when someone saves your site to a phone home screen.
  • Forgetting to clear the cache after updating. Browsers aggressively cache favicons, so after replacing one, you may need to hard-refresh or clear cached images before the new one shows up.
  • Placing the files in the wrong folder. The <link> tags need to point to wherever the files actually live on your server — a mismatch here is the most common reason a "correctly generated" favicon still doesn't appear.

Quick answers

Do I really need all those different sizes? For full compatibility across browsers and devices, yes — skipping some just means the icon won't display correctly in certain contexts, like when a phone user saves your site to their home screen.

Can I use a photo as a favicon? Technically yes, but photos rarely work well at such a small size — a simple graphic or logo mark reads much more clearly.

Will this cost anything? Generating the files is free with the right tool — you're not paying for design work, just automating an export process.
