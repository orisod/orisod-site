How to remove blank pages from a PDF

A scan from a document feeder often comes out with empty pages: the backs of single-sided sheets, a separator page, a page left over at the end. Removing them by hand means scrolling through the whole file. A blank-page check finds the likely ones for you, and you decide which to remove.

What counts as a blank page

A blank page is one with nothing a reader needs: no text, no picture, no drawing. In practice few blank pages are perfectly white. A scanned empty sheet is slightly grey, has a few specks of dust and sometimes a shadow along one edge. A page from a word processor may carry only a page number or a running header. Whether those pages count as blank depends on what the document is for, which is why the tool suggests pages and leaves the decision to you.

How visual detection works

Each page is drawn as a small picture in your browser. The tool works out the colour of that page's own paper, so an off-white or grey scan is compared with itself rather than with pure white. It then counts the marks that are clearly darker than the paper, leaving out a thin band along the edges where scanners leave shadows and punched holes. When those marks cover almost none of the page, the page is suggested for removal.

Why every marked page needs review

The check looks at a small picture, so it can't read what a faint mark means. A page that is empty apart from a signature line, a single handwritten initial or a light stamp can matter a great deal. A page marked as blank is a suggestion, not a decision: look at each one before you save, and clear Remove page on anything you want to keep.

Choosing the sensitivity

There are three settings. Conservative, the default, suggests only pages with practically no marks, so a page with a page number or a faint header is kept. Balanced also suggests pages with a few small marks, such as a page number. More aggressive goes further and ignores light marks and a wider band along the edges, which helps with noisy scans. Raising the sensitivity can add suggestions without removing earlier ones, and a page you chose yourself keeps your choice when you change it.

Scans, shadows and small marks

Scanners add things that aren't on the paper: dust, a grey tint, a dark edge where the lid didn't close, text showing through from the other side. If a scanned empty page isn't suggested at Conservative, try Balanced or More aggressive. If a page with a small but real mark is suggested, keep it by clearing its box, or go back to a lower setting.

Hidden text, annotations and forms

Some pages look empty but aren't. A page can hold white text, an invisible text layer added by text recognition, a comment, a form field or a signature field. The tool checks for these before suggesting a page, and a page that has any of them is not suggested automatically. It shows the note "This page looks blank but contains nonvisual content." You can still remove it yourself if you're sure.

What may change in the downloaded PDF

The tool removes the pages you chose and saves a new copy with the rest. Content, website links, comments and form fields on the pages you keep are generally retained, along with the title, author and attachments. Other things may change:

  • Bookmarks and internal links that pointed to a removed page may stop working.
  • Form fields whose box was on a removed page no longer appear on any page.
  • Tags for accessibility aren't updated, so a tagged PDF may need its tags checked.
  • PDF/A files may no longer meet the standard, even if the file still says it does.

Removing pages may affect bookmarks, internal links, forms, tags, attachments, PDF/A compliance and digital signatures. Check the downloaded PDF before replacing the original.

Password-protected and digitally signed PDFs

A PDF with a password can't be checked: remove the password in a trusted PDF application first, then add the file again. A digitally signed PDF can be checked, and the tool warns you first, because any change to a signed file, such as removing a page, means the signature may no longer be valid. If the signature matters, keep the signed original as it is.

Browser processing and privacy

Your PDF is processed in your browser and is not uploaded to Orisod servers. The tool loads its PDF libraries from orisod.com. It doesn't save your file, its name or your choices, and closing the tab removes them from the page.

Troubleshooting large PDFs

Every page has to be drawn once, so a long PDF can take a while and a file full of photos can use considerably more memory than its file size. For very large files, close other tabs or split the PDF into parts first. If the page becomes unresponsive, reload it and try a smaller part of the file.
