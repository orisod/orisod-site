How to Change a PDF's Page Size (A4, Letter, Legal or Custom)

A Letter PDF printed on A4 paper, or an A4 document printed on Letter, can produce unexpected margins or clipping. Before changing the file, decide whether the goal is to change the page boxes, scale the content proportionally, or prepare a print copy. Those operations are related but not identical.

Why this mismatch happens so often

Letter (used mainly in the US and Canada) and A4 (used almost everywhere else) are close in size but not identical — Letter is slightly wider and shorter, A4 slightly narrower and taller. A document created in one region and opened in another routinely carries the "wrong" page size for whoever's printing it, and the same mismatch shows up with Legal-sized paper for contracts or forms.

What resizing actually does

A resizing tool may place proportionally scaled content inside a new page size, stretch it to fill, crop it, or change only the page boxes. These choices produce different results when the source and target have different aspect ratios. Proportional fitting avoids distortion but can leave margins; filling the page may crop or distort content. Confirm the tool's behavior before processing the document.

Standard sizes vs. custom dimensions

A4, Letter, and Legal cover common office needs, while printers and booklet workflows may specify custom dimensions. Check the measurement unit and page orientation. A mixed-size PDF may also require different treatment per page rather than forcing every page through one scale and orientation.

What else can change

Page count and order can remain the same, but the PDF itself is modified. Depending on the implementation, page boxes, annotations, links, forms, bookmarks, accessibility structure, and digital signatures may be affected. File size can increase or decrease if content is rewritten, rasterized, or recompressed; it is not guaranteed to stay close to the original.

Letter to A4 without guessing

Suppose a Letter contract must be printed on A4. First try the print dialog's fit-to-page option, which can produce the needed paper copy without altering the PDF. Create a resized PDF only when the recipient, archive, or downstream system specifically requires A4 pages. Then inspect margins, signatures, form fields, and page orientation.

Checks before saving the resized copy

Will resizing distort content? Proportional scaling should not distort it, but may add margins. Stretch-to-fill can distort it, and fill-and-crop can remove edges. The A4-to-Letter difference is still large enough to matter for forms, borders, and precise layouts.

Will file size change? It depends on how the tool rewrites the PDF. Scaling vector objects may have little effect; rasterization or image recompression can change the size substantially.

Can I use a size that isn't A4, Letter or Legal? Yes, most resizing tools support a custom width and height alongside the standard presets.
