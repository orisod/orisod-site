How to Trim the Margins on a PDF (Without Reprinting It)

A scanned page comes out with two inches of dead white space around the edges, or a PDF exported from some other tool has margins that don't match what you actually need. Redoing the source — rescanning, re-exporting, reformatting — is the slow way to fix it. Trimming the existing file's margins directly is the fast one.

Why PDFs end up with wide or uneven margins

Margins get baked into a PDF for all kinds of reasons that have nothing to do with what you actually want: a scanner defaults to capturing the full glass bed instead of just the page, a document was designed for a different paper size than it's now being used for, or an export setting added padding nobody asked for. None of these are content problems — the page itself is fine — the visible boundary around it is just larger than it needs to be.

What cropping a PDF actually changes

Cropping moves a page's visible boundary inward by the amount you specify on each side, typically measured in millimeters. It doesn't touch, redraw, or recompress anything inside that boundary — text stays selectable, images stay at their original resolution. Think of it less as "editing" the page and more as changing which part of it gets shown and printed.

Choosing the right margins for your case

Uneven margins on all four sides is common with scanned documents, so top, bottom, left and right usually need different values rather than one uniform number. A quick way to figure out what you need: open the PDF, look at how much empty space sits along each edge, and estimate in millimeters — most crop tools let you preview or re-adjust before saving, so getting it exactly right on the first try isn't necessary.

What stays unchanged

Cropping is a boundary change, not a content edit — the underlying text, images and layout inside the new boundary are exactly what they were before. If your PDF has multiple pages of different sizes, most tools apply the same margin measurements to every page rather than customizing per page, so watch for an error if a page turns out too small for the margins you set.

A concrete example

You scan a receipt or a single-page form on a flatbed scanner set to capture a full A4 area, but the actual content only fills the top third of the page. Instead of rescanning at a tighter crop setting, you'd trim roughly 150mm off the bottom and a smaller amount off the sides — one pass, and the result is a page sized to the content instead of the scanner bed.

Quick answers

Does cropping delete any content permanently? No — it moves the page's visible boundary inward. The content underneath isn't redrawn or rasterized, so nothing is lost or degraded.

Can I crop different amounts from each side? Yes — top, bottom, left and right are independent, so uneven margins are easy to fix without over-trimming a side that was already fine.

Will this work on a multi-page PDF? Yes, the same margin measurements apply to every page in one pass — useful for a scanned document where every page shares the same scanner-bed margin problem.
